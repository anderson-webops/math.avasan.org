# Algebra 1A practice self-checks

## Placement practice

1. 3x=15, so x=5; 3(5)+7=22.
2. Horizontal change is 3 and vertical change is 6; slope=6/3=2.
3. x+1=7-x gives 2x=6, so (x,y)=(3,4); 4=3+1 and 4=7-3.

## Properties practice

1. 4x-12: the factor 4 multiplies both x and -3.
2. 5x+3. At x=-1 the original is -2+5-3-2=-2, matching -5+3=-2.
3. It does not: 3(2)+4=10, while the right side is 13. The actual solution is x=3.

## Single-step practice

1. Add 7: x=-5, and -5-7=-12.
2. Divide by -4: x=-5, and -4(-5)=20.
3. Multiply both sides by 5: x=-15; -15/5=-3. Multiplying by the nonzero divisor reverses the division.

## Multi-step practice

1. 3x-2=2x+9 gives x=11; the original sides are 31.
2. All real numbers: distribution gives the identity 2x+2=2x+2.
3. No solution: subtracting 2x gives 2=5. The resulting 0x=3 is impossible; division by zero is undefined.

## First check-in practice

1. 12+4n=36 gives n=6 items; 12+4(6)=36.
2. 8h<=50 gives h<=6.25. The largest whole number is 6: $48 fits, while 7 hours cost $56.
3. -2x=12, so x=-6; 5-2(-6)=17. Multiplying two negative numbers contributes +12.

## Slope practice

1. (-3-5)/(2-(-2))=-8/4=-2; reversing both differences gives 8/(-4)=-2.
2. The first is vertical x=3 and has undefined slope because its horizontal change is zero. The second is horizontal y=7 with slope 0.
3. (35-50)/(5-2)=-5 liters per minute. Continuing that constant rate gives 30 liters at minute 6.

## Community data practice

1. Each monthly change is 3; v=3m+5 matches all four records.
2. Rates are 3,4,5 visits per month. They vary, so one exact straight line cannot contain all four points.
3. v(5)=20 visits if the pattern continues. A pattern in these fictional observations does not establish a cause.

## Slope-intercept practice

1. y=3x-4; points (0,-4) and (2,2).
2. The fixed fee is $5 and the rate is $2 per kilometer. F(7)=19 dollars.
3. Slope=(7-(-1))/4=2 and intercept=-1, so y=2x-1; it gives -1 at 0 and 7 at 4.

## Point-slope practice

1. y-5=-3(x-2), so y=-3x+11.
2. Slope=-8/4=-2; y-4=-2(x+1), hence y=-2x+2. At x=-1 it gives 4; at x=3 it gives -4.
3. y+2=(x-4)/2, so y=x/2-4. The slopes agree and the intercepts -4 and 6 differ.

## Inequality practice

1. -3x<=9 gives x>=-3. At -3 the left side is 15, at 0 it is 6, and at -4 it is 18.
2. Use a dashed y=2x-1 line and shade above it; boundary points are excluded. (0,0) passes because 0>-1.
3. 4+5n<=24 gives n<=4; with the stated domain, n can be 0,1,2,3,4.

## Graphing systems practice

1. For y=2x, use (0,0),(2,4); for y=-x+6, use (0,6),(2,4). The intersection is (2,4), satisfying both.
2. No solution: equal slopes and distinct intercepts give parallel lines.
3. Infinitely many solutions: dividing the second equation by 2 yields the first. Both contain (0,-2) and (1,1).

## Substitution practice

1. 5x-2=13 gives x=3,y=7. Checks: 7=9-2 and 6+7=13.
2. 3(2y+1)-y=13 gives 5y=10, y=2,x=5. Checks: 5=2(2)+1 and 15-2=13.
3. Let L+S=11 and 2L+3S=27. Substituting L=11-S gives S=5 dollars, L=6 dollars; 6+5=11 and 12+15=27.

## Elimination practice

1. Subtracting gives 4y=8, so y=2 and x=3. Checks: 6+6=12 and 6-2=4.
2. Scale by 3 and 2: 9x+6y=36 and 4x-6y=-10. Then 13x=26, x=2,y=3. Checks: 6+6=12 and 4-9=-5.
3. a+c=20 and 8a+5c=130. Subtract 5(a+c)=100 to get 3a=30; a=10,c=10. Counts total 20 and revenue is 80+50=130 dollars.

## Capstone review practice

1. Equality at h=3 hours and $18. For h>3, A is cheaper because B's hourly rate is larger; the cost difference B-A=2h-6 is positive.
2. 60-7w>=25 gives w<=5. With w>=0 whole, the allowed values are 0 through 5; week 5 has $25 and week 6 has $18.
3. Slope=6/3=2; y-5=2(x-1) and y=2x+3. The points (1,5),(2,7),(4,11) satisfy the rule; equal input steps have equal output changes.

## Signed division

1. Dividing by 5 gives -x=6, not x=6. The full coefficient is -5.
2. Divide by -5 to get x=-6.
3. At 6 the left side is -30, which fails. At -6 it is 30, which equals the right side.

## Film budget units

1. Each unit rate must be multiplied by its matching quantity before adding dollar amounts.
2. Production costs 150(4)=600, props 25(8)=200, marketing 32.50(6)=195 and editing 400 dollars. Total=$1,395.
3. Remaining=$105. An extra producer-hour costs $150, so the budget allows zero extra whole producer-hours; $105/150=0.7 is not a whole hour.

## Strict budget threshold

1. Division by -20 reverses the inequality; d must be greater than 6.
2. d>6, so the first qualifying whole-number day is 7.
3. Balances are $50,$30,$10. Day 6 is exactly the threshold and fails a strict 'below' condition; day 7 qualifies.

## Matching slope differences

1. The numerator uses A minus B but the denominator uses B minus A. Both differences must use the same order.
2. (-3-5)/(2-(-2))=-2. Using A gives y-5=-2(x+2), or y=-2x+1.
3. At -2 the rule gives 5; at 2 it gives -3. The negative slope means the line falls as x increases.

## Fixed fees and rates

1. 12 is the starting fee, while 3 is dollars per hour.
2. C=12+3h. The costs are $12 at zero hours and $27 at five hours.
3. (0,12) and (5,27) give slope (27-12)/(5-0)=3 dollars per hour.

## Negative point coordinates

1. The x-coordinate is -3, so x-x1 is x-(-3)=x+3.
2. y-4=2(x+3), hence y=2x+10.
3. The flawed rule gives -8 at x=-3; the corrected rule gives 4. Only the corrected line contains the supplied point.

## Boundary and shading

1. The <= sign includes equality, so the line is solid and (0,4) belongs.
2. 0<=4 is true, so shade the half-plane containing (0,0), below the boundary.
3. (0,5) fails because 5<=4 is false.

## Listed observations and strict thresholds

1. 40% equals the threshold; it is not strictly below 40%.
2. Day 5 is the first listed qualifying day. Average change from day 2 to day 4 is (40-60)/(4-2)=-10 percentage points per day.
3. A day-3 value of 50% requires linear interpolation. An interval average alone does not prove a constant daily change or describe conditions beyond this fictional table.

## Parallel through a given point

1. Parallel nonvertical lines share their slope; their intercepts can differ.
2. 1=-4(-1)+b gives b=-3. The new equation is y=-4x-3.
3. At x=-1 the new line gives 1; the old gives 10. Equal slopes with different intercepts mean no intersection.

## One constraint is insufficient

1. 3=2(1)+1 is true, but 3=-1+7 is false because the right side is 6.
2. 2x+1=-x+7 gives x=2,y=5. Both rules contain (2,5).
3. A system requires both constraints simultaneously. A point on just one line need not lie on the other.

## Substituting the whole expression

1. The replacement for y is the entire expression 2x-3.
2. 4x+(2x-3)=15 gives 6x=18, so x=3,y=3.
3. The flawed pair satisfies y=2x-3 but 4(2.5)+2=12, not 15. For (3,3), 3=6-3 and 12+3=15.

## Two-type inventory

1. Twice the first equation is 2x+2y=92, so the right-side difference is 118-92.
2. y=26 and x=20.
3. 20+26=46 and 2(20)+3(26)=118. If y were 72, x would be -26, violating the stated domain and cost equation.

## Publication quota model

1. A=12+3m and B=6+5m; starting totals are the intercepts.
2. 12+3m=6+5m gives m=3 months and 21 papers for each lab.
3. B-A=2m-6, so B>A when m>3. The first whole-number month is 4; B gains two more papers per month than A.

## Comparing plans over a domain

1. The two rates differ, so the cost difference changes with the input.
2. 20+2n=5+5n gives n=5 and a cost of $30. B is cheaper for n<5 and A for n>5.
3. At 2 visits, A=$24 and B=$15; at 8, A=$36 and B=$45. B is cheaper for 0 through 4 visits, both tie at 5, and A is cheaper for 6 or more visits.
